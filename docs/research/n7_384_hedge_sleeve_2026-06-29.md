# N7 384 profile + small-N hedge sleeve

- Base portfolio: `b384_dd32_smooth80_convex19` from `results/n7_sleeve_mix_capital_20260629/sleeve_mix_issue_profit.csv`.
- Hedge snapshot: `1h`; rolling train issues: `12`.
- Profiles tested: `648`; hedge units: `8,16,32,64`.
- Hedge construction uses only prior issues for model fitting and the selected odds snapshot for current issue prices.

## Interpretation

- Decision: do not promote this hedge sleeve into the current operating
  profile. The measured improvement is too small relative to the added
  complexity and profit drag.
- The useful hedge signal is concentrated in `N=2`, not `N=3`.
- The best logical hedge is the `near` bucket: 1h odds rank 3, odds >= 5, model probability 5%-25%, model/market ratio >= 1.0, and `model_p * odds` in `[0.90, 1.063)`. This matches the intended "high SP, low win-rate, not good enough for the main high-N selector" leg.
- Keeping total budget at 384, a 32-unit N2 near hedge improves maxDD from `-15077` to `-14713` units and keeps recent 2023-2025 profit roughly flat, but lowers full-period profit per 256 from `241249` to `235668`.
- A 64-unit same-budget hedge improves maxDD further to `-14349` units, but profit per 256 drops to `230088`.
- Adding an external 64-unit hedge sleeve gives the strongest maxDD reduction, to `-13573` units, but profit per 256 drops materially to `205539`; this should be treated as defensive capital, not a return enhancer.
- The high-activity `value` bucket can save more full-loss issues, but it does not improve maxDD. It is better classified as a "ticket feel" smoother than as drawdown control.

Interpretation: N=3 has the wrong tradeoff for this hedge role. Tight/high-odds
N3 barely fires and has zero hit contribution in the backtest. Broad/value N3
fires often enough to save a few full-loss issues, but the added losing tickets
more than offset that benefit and maxDD gets worse.
